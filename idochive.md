## 1. Executive Summary

iDocHive is Creation Next's governed enterprise document and knowledge intelligence platform for organizations with large, sensitive, and fragmented document estates. It is sold for customer-controlled, on-premise, private, or air-gapped deployment. It is not a public multi-tenant SaaS product.

It converts paper and digital documents into permission-aware, searchable, traceable, AI-ready organizational knowledge.

The core transformation is:

> PAPER -> DATA -> INFORMATION -> INTELLIGENCE

iDocHive combines document management, OCR, structured extraction, enterprise search, workflow automation, and private AI within the customer's controlled environment.

The strongest positioning is:

> iDocHive is the governed enterprise document and knowledge intelligence layer for sovereign AI.

Short market message:

> Private AI for your enterprise documents.

Core promise:

> Your documents. Your data. Your private intelligence.

iDocHive is designed for government agencies, ministries, regulated enterprises, archives, construction and infrastructure programs, healthcare organizations, financial institutions, and other security-first organizations.

## 3. The Problem

Large organizations often hold decades of knowledge in:

- Paper archives
- Scanned image files
- PDFs and office documents
- Email attachments
- Shared drives
- Departmental repositories
- Legacy applications
- Unstructured project records
- Contracts, policies, reports, drawings, and correspondence

This creates operational problems:

- Employees spend hours locating documents and answers
- Poor scans and inconsistent metadata reduce search quality
- Manual data entry increases cost and error rates
- Departments create duplicate records and disconnected workflows
- Access permissions are difficult to enforce consistently
- Knowledge leaves when employees leave
- AI projects lack clean, governed, permission-aware source material
- Public AI services create privacy, residency, and security concerns
- Decisions lack clear source evidence and auditability

The strategic gap is clear:

> Sovereign AI infrastructure still needs a governed bridge to the documents where organizational knowledge already exists.

## 6. Core Design Principles

### Privacy by architecture

Confidential information stays inside the customer's approved boundary.

### Governance before generation

AI access follows document permissions, information policies, and approval rules.

### Source validation

AI responses must point back to source material. The user must be able to inspect the evidence.

### Human accountability

High-impact outputs require human review and approval. iDocHive supports decisions. It does not remove accountable decision owners.

### Model independence

iDocHive should support different approved models and inference environments. The organization should not depend on one external model provider.

### Enterprise integration

iDocHive should connect with identity, security, records, workflow, storage, backup, and line-of-business systems.

### Deployment flexibility

The same product direction should support pilot, private cloud, on-premise, highly available, and air-gapped deployments.

## 7. Sovereign and Air-Gapped Deployment

The sovereign deployment baseline includes:

- Customer-controlled infrastructure boundary
- No production dependency on the public internet
- No external AI API requirement
- No uncontrolled document or telemetry path
- Local or customer-approved model inference
- Customer-controlled encryption keys
- Customer-controlled identity and access policies
- Customer-controlled logging and retention
- Signed and scanned offline software updates for isolated environments

The following remain within the customer's controlled environment:

- Original documents
- Extracted text and metadata
- Embeddings and indexes
- Model weights
- Prompts and responses
- Encryption keys
- User activity and audit events
- Workflow and approval records

### Deployment patterns

#### Pilot or departmental deployment

- Docker Compose
- Limited document collection
- Defined user group
- Measurable acceptance criteria
- Controlled production transition

#### Enterprise high-availability deployment

- K3s or Kubernetes
- Redundant application services
- Highly available database and storage
- Internal load balancing
- Central monitoring and SIEM integration
- Defined backup, recovery, and disaster-recovery controls

#### Air-gapped deployment

- No internet access in production
- Internal model serving
- Offline installation and update packages
- Controlled media scanning and approval
- Internal observability and security monitoring
- Local documentation and operational runbooks

## 9. Permission-Aware AI Flow

1. The user authenticates through the approved identity system.
2. iDocHive resolves the user's roles, department, groups, and record permissions.
3. The user submits a search or AI question.
4. Retrieval searches only authorized documents and approved collections.
5. The system ranks relevant passages.
6. The approved model generates a response from the retrieved evidence.
7. The response includes source references.
8. The system logs the query, retrieved evidence, model, prompt policy, response, and user action.
9. High-impact actions enter a human approval workflow.

## 38. Ingestion-to-Retrieval Reference Flow

1. User or service uploads or bulk-imports a document.
2. Documents Service stores the encrypted original in object storage.
3. The system writes an ingestion audit event.
4. Documents Service enqueues the processing job.
5. OCR service extracts text and layout.
6. The model pipeline classifies, extracts, summarizes, or translates according to policy.
7. Embedding service writes approved vectors to the vector store.
8. Structured AI output is stored as `pending_review`.
9. The system logs model version, confidence, prompt policy, inputs, and result metadata.
10. Workflow routes review when the document class requires approval.
11. The reviewer receives an assigned task.
12. The reviewer approves, rejects, edits, or requests changes.
13. The system writes `approved_by`, approval timestamp, and decision evidence.
14. The system logs the human approval event.
15. An authorized user submits a search or retrieval request.
16. The API applies RBAC and content filters at query time.
17. Hybrid search combines exact and semantic results and returns citations to approved content.

## 48. Objection Handling

### "We already have SharePoint or another DMS."

Response:

> iDocHive does not need to replace every existing repository. It can govern the document-intelligence workflow around selected sensitive archives, connect approved sources, and return permission-aware answers. The assessment will determine whether integration, migration, or a controlled coexistence model creates the lowest risk.

### "Why not use a public AI assistant?"

Response:

> The decision depends on data classification and policy. iDocHive is designed for content that must remain within customer-controlled infrastructure. Retrieval permissions, models, prompts, responses, keys, and audit events remain under the customer's controls.

### "Your company is smaller than the established vendors."

Response:

> Creation Next provides direct product engineering, local adaptation, and legacy integration around one sovereign use case. We start with a paid, measurable pilot and contract acceptance criteria before enterprise rollout.

### "Air-gapped AI will be slow or expensive."

Response:

> iDocHive routes work across OCR engines, small language models, custom transformers, and asynchronous queues. The pilot measures quality, capacity, and hardware needs on the buyer's own document types before production sizing.

### "How do we trust AI answers?"

Response:

> Answers include source evidence. Retrieval follows user permissions. Controlled outputs remain pending until a human approves them. The audit trail links the source, model version, policy, response, reviewer, and decision.

### "Does iDocHive make us compliant?"

Response:

> No software creates compliance by itself. iDocHive provides controls and evidence that support the customer's compliance program. The final mapping depends on the country's law, sector, deployment, and operating procedures.
